# Heart rate, conduction and QT interactions

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype. Sources: FDA prescribing information for digoxin (Lanoxin), amiodarone (Cordarone), verapamil, diltiazem, citalopram (Celexa) and clarithromycin, Warnings and Drug Interactions sections; FDA Drug Safety Communications on citalopram and QT prolongation (2011, revised 2012).

## Digoxin + amiodarone
Amiodarone inhibits P-glycoprotein and reduces renal and non-renal clearance of digoxin, so digoxin levels can rise substantially. Digoxin toxicity causes nausea, visual disturbance, bradycardia and arrhythmias. Labels advise reducing the digoxin dose when amiodarone is started and monitoring digoxin levels.

## Digoxin + verapamil
Verapamil increases digoxin concentrations and both drugs slow conduction through the AV node. The combination can cause digoxin toxicity, marked bradycardia or heart block.

## Digoxin + clarithromycin
Clarithromycin inhibits P-glycoprotein and can raise digoxin levels, causing toxicity. An alternative antibiotic or digoxin level monitoring should be considered.

## Digoxin + loop diuretics (furosemide)
Loop diuretics can cause hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia, which increase sensitivity to digoxin and the risk of arrhythmias. Electrolytes and digoxin levels should be monitored.

## Verapamil or diltiazem + beta-blockers
Non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers and beta-blockers (metoprolol, bisoprolol, atenolol) both slow the heart rate and AV conduction and reduce contractility. Verapamil with a beta-blocker in particular can cause severe bradycardia, AV block, hypotension and heart failure. The combination needs close monitoring of heart rate and blood pressure. Amlodipine, a dihydropyridine, does not share this effect.

## Amiodarone + beta-blockers
Amiodarone slows the heart rate and AV conduction; with a beta-blocker this can cause symptomatic bradycardia or AV block. Heart rate should be monitored.

## Citalopram + amiodarone (QT prolongation)
Citalopram causes dose-dependent QT-interval prolongation, and FDA advises a maximum of 20 mg per day in patients over 60. Amiodarone also prolongs the QT interval. Together they increase the risk of torsades de pointes. An alternative antidepressant or ECG monitoring should be considered.

## Citalopram + haloperidol
Both drugs can prolong the QT interval; ECG monitoring should be considered, especially in older adults or with electrolyte disturbance.

## Clarithromycin + amiodarone
Clarithromycin prolongs the QT interval and inhibits CYP3A4, which metabolises amiodarone. The combination increases the risk of torsades de pointes; an alternative antibiotic such as one without QT effects should be considered.
