# Narrow-therapeutic-index drugs: lithium and methotrexate

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype. Sources: FDA prescribing information for lithium and methotrexate, Warnings and Drug Interactions sections; STOPP/START version 3.

## Lithium + NSAIDs
NSAIDs reduce renal lithium clearance and can raise lithium levels into the toxic range (tremor, confusion, ataxia, kidney injury). If an NSAID is necessary, lithium levels should be monitored closely; otherwise an alternative analgesic is preferred.

## Lithium + ACE inhibitors or ARBs
ACE inhibitors and ARBs reduce lithium excretion and can cause lithium toxicity, sometimes weeks after the combination is started. Lithium levels should be monitored closely.

## Lithium + thiazide diuretics
Thiazides such as hydrochlorothiazide reduce sodium excretion and increase renal reabsorption of lithium, raising lithium levels. The lithium label warns about this combination; levels should be monitored and the dose reviewed.

## Methotrexate + trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
Trimethoprim is an antifolate and also reduces renal clearance of methotrexate. Together they can cause severe, sometimes fatal bone-marrow suppression (pancytopenia), even with low-dose weekly methotrexate. The combination should be avoided and escalated for prescriber review.

## Methotrexate + NSAIDs
NSAIDs reduce renal elimination of methotrexate and can cause methotrexate toxicity (bone-marrow suppression, gastrointestinal and kidney toxicity). The risk is greatest with high-dose methotrexate; with low-dose regimens the combination is sometimes used with monitoring of blood counts and kidney function.
