# Potentially inappropriate medications in older adults (Beers-style summary)

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype, paraphrasing selected recommendations. Source: American Geriatrics Society 2023 Updated AGS Beers Criteria for Potentially Inappropriate Medication Use in Older Adults, J Am Geriatr Soc 2023;71:2052-2081; FDA prescribing information for levothyroxine. The Beers Criteria apply to adults aged 65 and older and are intended to support, not replace, clinical judgement.

## Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs
Older adults are more sensitive to benzodiazepines (diazepam, lorazepam, alprazolam) and metabolise long-acting agents slowly. They increase the risk of cognitive impairment, delirium, falls, fractures and motor-vehicle crashes. Z-drugs such as zolpidem have similar adverse effects with minimal improvement in sleep. Both are on the "avoid" list.

## Strongly anticholinergic drugs
First-generation antihistamines (diphenhydramine), tricyclic antidepressants (amitriptyline) and bladder antimuscarinics (oxybutynin) are strongly anticholinergic. They can cause confusion, delirium, dry mouth, constipation and urinary retention. Using two or more anticholinergic drugs adds to the cumulative anticholinergic burden and should be avoided.

## Antipsychotics
Antipsychotics (quetiapine, haloperidol) increase the risk of stroke, cognitive decline and mortality in people with dementia. They should be avoided for behavioural problems of dementia or delirium unless non-pharmacological options have failed and the patient may harm themselves or others.

## NSAIDs and aspirin
Chronic NSAID use should be avoided unless alternatives are not effective and a gastroprotective agent is used. Aspirin should not be started for primary prevention of cardiovascular disease.

## Sulfonylureas
Sulfonylureas should be avoided as first- or second-line diabetes therapy because of the risk of prolonged hypoglycaemia.

## Digoxin and amiodarone
Digoxin should be avoided as first-line therapy for atrial fibrillation or heart failure; if used, doses above 0.125 mg per day should be avoided because reduced kidney clearance increases toxicity. Amiodarone should be avoided as first-line therapy for atrial fibrillation unless the patient has heart failure or substantial left-ventricular hypertrophy.

## Anticoagulants
Warfarin should not be started as first-line therapy for atrial fibrillation or venous thromboembolism unless DOACs are contraindicated, and rivaroxaban should be avoided for long-term treatment in favour of safer alternatives.

## Proton pump inhibitors
Scheduled PPI use (omeprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole) for more than 8 weeks should be avoided unless there is a clear indication (for example high-risk patients on oral corticosteroids or chronic NSAIDs, Barrett's oesophagus, erosive oesophagitis), because of the risk of C. difficile infection, bone loss and fractures.

## Levothyroxine + proton pump inhibitors
Levothyroxine absorption depends on gastric acidity. Drugs that raise gastric pH, such as PPIs, can reduce absorption; TSH should be monitored if long-term PPI therapy is started. This is usually a minor interaction.
