# Renin-angiotensin system drugs and hyperkalaemia

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype. Sources: FDA prescribing information for ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone (Aldactone) and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim), Warnings and Drug Interactions sections; ONTARGET Investigators, N Engl J Med 2008;358:1547-1559; STOPP/START version 3.

## ACE inhibitors or ARBs + spironolactone
ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, ramipril, enalapril), ARBs (losartan, valsartan) and spironolactone all reduce potassium excretion. Together they can cause serious hyperkalaemia, particularly with reduced kidney function, dehydration or older age. The combination is used deliberately in heart failure, but potassium and kidney function must be monitored.

## ACE inhibitors, ARBs or spironolactone + potassium supplements
Adding potassium chloride to a drug that retains potassium increases the risk of hyperkalaemia. Labels for ACE inhibitors and ARBs warn about potassium supplements, and potassium supplements are generally not used with spironolactone. The ongoing need for the supplement should be reviewed and potassium monitored.

## ACE inhibitor + ARB (dual RAS blockade)
Combining an ACE inhibitor with an ARB blocks the renin-angiotensin system twice. In the ONTARGET trial the combination did not improve cardiovascular outcomes and increased hypotension, hyperkalaemia and kidney dysfunction. Product labels warn against dual blockade; it is generally not recommended.

## Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole + ACE inhibitors or spironolactone
Trimethoprim blocks sodium channels in the distal nephron (an amiloride-like effect) and reduces potassium excretion. With ACE inhibitors, and especially with spironolactone, this can cause clinically significant hyperkalaemia within days. Potassium should be monitored or an alternative antibiotic chosen.

## Two ACE inhibitors or two ARBs
Taking two drugs from the same class (for example lisinopril and ramipril) is a therapeutic duplication that increases hypotension, hyperkalaemia and kidney risk.
