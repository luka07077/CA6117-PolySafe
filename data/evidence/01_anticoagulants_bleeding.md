# Anticoagulants and bleeding risk (warfarin and DOACs)

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype. Sources: FDA prescribing information for warfarin (Coumadin), apixaban (Eliquis), rivaroxaban (Xarelto) and dabigatran (Pradaxa), Drug Interactions and Warnings sections; American Geriatrics Society 2023 Updated AGS Beers Criteria (J Am Geriatr Soc 2023;71:2052-2081).

## Warfarin + NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, celecoxib)
NSAIDs inhibit platelet function and damage the gastric and duodenal mucosa. Taken with warfarin they add to the anticoagulant effect and substantially increase the risk of serious bleeding, most often from the gastrointestinal tract. The warfarin label lists NSAIDs among drugs that increase bleeding risk, and the Beers Criteria advise avoiding the combination in older adults. If an NSAID cannot be avoided, the INR and signs of bleeding should be monitored and gastroprotection considered. Paracetamol (acetaminophen) at usual doses is generally preferred for pain, although regular high doses can also raise the INR.

## Warfarin + aspirin or clopidogrel (antiplatelet agents)
Combining an anticoagulant with an antiplatelet drug adds two different antihaemostatic mechanisms and increases the risk of major bleeding. There are specific indications (for example a recent coronary stent with atrial fibrillation), but the combination should be intentional, time-limited and reviewed regularly. Low-dose aspirin taken for primary prevention alongside warfarin is a common source of avoidable bleeding.

## Warfarin + amiodarone
Amiodarone inhibits CYP2C9 and CYP3A4, the enzymes that clear warfarin. The INR typically rises over several weeks and the effect persists long after amiodarone is stopped because of its very long half-life. The warfarin dose usually needs to be reduced and the INR monitored closely.

## Warfarin + fluconazole, metronidazole or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
These anti-infectives inhibit CYP2C9, the main pathway for the more potent S-enantiomer of warfarin. Even a short course can cause a marked rise in INR and bleeding. The INR should be checked during and after the course, or an alternative anti-infective chosen.

## Warfarin + ciprofloxacin or clarithromycin
Fluoroquinolones and macrolides can raise the INR in some patients through reduced warfarin clearance and changes in vitamin-K-producing gut flora. The effect is less predictable; INR monitoring is advised when the antibiotic starts or stops.

## Warfarin + levothyroxine
Thyroid hormone increases the breakdown of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors. Starting or increasing levothyroxine can increase the anticoagulant response, so the INR should be monitored when the thyroid dose changes.

## DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran) + NSAIDs or antiplatelets
Direct oral anticoagulants have fewer metabolic interactions than warfarin, but pharmacodynamic interactions remain. Their labels warn that NSAIDs, aspirin, clopidogrel and other antiplatelet agents increase bleeding risk. Concomitant use should have a clear indication and be monitored for bleeding.

## Two anticoagulants at the same time
Using two anticoagulants together (for example warfarin plus apixaban, or two DOACs) is almost never intended outside a supervised switch between agents. It greatly increases the risk of major bleeding and should be treated as a critical finding needing immediate prescriber review.

## Anticoagulant + antiplatelet + NSAID ("triple" bleeding risk)
When an anticoagulant, an antiplatelet drug and an NSAID are all taken together, three separate mechanisms impair haemostasis. This regimen carries a very high risk of major bleeding and needs urgent review.

## Older adults
The 2023 Beers Criteria recommend against starting warfarin as first-line therapy for atrial fibrillation or venous thromboembolism unless DOACs are contraindicated, and recommend avoiding rivaroxaban for long-term treatment because of higher bleeding rates than alternatives such as apixaban.
