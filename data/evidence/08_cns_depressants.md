# CNS depressants: opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids and hypnotics

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype. Sources: FDA Drug Safety Communication (August 2016): boxed warnings for combined use of opioids and benzodiazepines; FDA Drug Safety Communication (December 2019): serious breathing difficulties with gabapentin and pregabalin; AGS Beers Criteria 2023 (drug-drug interactions to avoid in older adults); STOPP/START version 3.

## Opioids + benzodiazepines (boxed warning)
Combining an opioid (tramadol, codeine, oxycodone, morphine) with a benzodiazepine (diazepam, lorazepam, alprazolam) causes additive CNS and respiratory depression. In 2016 FDA added boxed warnings to both drug classes about profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death with concomitant use. The combination should be reserved for patients without alternative options, at the lowest effective doses and shortest duration, with monitoring. The Beers Criteria advise avoiding it. PolySafe treats it as a critical finding that must be escalated.

## Opioids + gabapentin or pregabalin
In 2019 FDA warned that gabapentinoids can cause serious breathing difficulties in patients with respiratory risk factors, including those taking opioids or other CNS depressants and older adults. The Beers Criteria advise avoiding this combination except when switching from opioid therapy or when used to reduce opioid dose.

## Opioids or benzodiazepines + zolpidem
Z-drugs such as zolpidem act on the same GABA-A receptor as benzodiazepines. With opioids they add CNS and respiratory depression. With benzodiazepines they duplicate sedative-hypnotic therapy and increase the risk of excessive sedation, falls and fractures. Duplicate hypnotic therapy is rarely appropriate.

## Three or more CNS-active drugs in older adults
The Beers Criteria recommend avoiding any combination of three or more CNS-active drugs (antiepileptics including gabapentinoids, antidepressants, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, opioids and skeletal muscle relaxants) because of the increased risk of falls and fractures. The number of CNS-active drugs should be reduced where possible.

## Two benzodiazepines or two opioids
Taking two benzodiazepines, or two opioids, at the same time is a therapeutic duplication. It increases sedation and respiratory-depression risk and should be confirmed as intentional (for example a long-acting plus a short-acting opioid under specialist supervision).
