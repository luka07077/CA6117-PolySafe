# Statins, colchicine and CYP3A4 inhibitors

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype. Sources: FDA prescribing information for simvastatin (Zocor), atorvastatin (Lipitor) and colchicine (Colcrys), Contraindications, Dosage and Drug Interactions sections; FDA Drug Safety Communication (2011): new restrictions, contraindications and dose limitations for simvastatin.

## Simvastatin + clarithromycin (contraindicated)
Simvastatin is extensively metabolised by CYP3A4. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, including clarithromycin, raise simvastatin exposure many-fold and increase the risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. The simvastatin label lists strong CYP3A4 inhibitors as contraindicated. This combination should be escalated for prescriber review before the next dose; options include withholding simvastatin during the antibiotic course or choosing a different antibiotic.

## Atorvastatin + clarithromycin
Atorvastatin is also a CYP3A4 substrate, but less sensitive than simvastatin. The label advises caution and a dose limit while clarithromycin is taken.

## Simvastatin dose limits with amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil and amiodarone
The 2011 FDA communication introduced dose limits because these drugs raise simvastatin levels: with amlodipine or amiodarone, simvastatin should not exceed 20 mg per day; with diltiazem or verapamil, it should not exceed 10 mg per day. The combination is not contraindicated, but the simvastatin dose should be reviewed and patients counselled about muscle pain or weakness.

## Statins + colchicine
Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis have been reported when colchicine is combined with statins, possibly from additive muscle toxicity. Patients should be monitored for muscle symptoms, especially older adults and those with reduced kidney function.

## Colchicine + clarithromycin
Clarithromycin inhibits both CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein and can greatly increase colchicine concentrations. Fatal colchicine toxicity has been reported. The colchicine label contraindicates this combination in patients with renal or hepatic impairment and requires a dose reduction otherwise.

## Colchicine + verapamil or diltiazem
These calcium-channel blockers inhibit CYP3A4 (and verapamil also P-glycoprotein), raising colchicine levels. The colchicine dose should be reviewed.

## Two statins at the same time
Taking two statins (for example simvastatin and atorvastatin) is a therapeutic duplication. It adds myopathy risk without additional benefit and usually reflects a switch that was not completed.
