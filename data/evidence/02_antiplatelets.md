# Antiplatelet agents (aspirin, clopidogrel)

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype. Sources: FDA prescribing information for clopidogrel (Plavix), Drug Interactions and Warnings sections; FDA information on concomitant use of ibuprofen and low-dose aspirin; AGS Beers Criteria 2023.

## Clopidogrel + omeprazole or esomeprazole
Clopidogrel is a prodrug that needs CYP2C19 to form its active metabolite. Omeprazole and esomeprazole inhibit CYP2C19 and reduce the active metabolite and the antiplatelet effect. The clopidogrel label advises avoiding concomitant use of omeprazole or esomeprazole. When gastroprotection is needed, a PPI with less CYP2C19 inhibition such as pantoprazole is commonly chosen after review.

## Clopidogrel + NSAIDs
NSAIDs add gastrointestinal mucosal injury to the antiplatelet effect of clopidogrel, increasing the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding. The need for the NSAID should be reviewed and gastroprotection considered.

## Aspirin + NSAIDs (for example ibuprofen)
NSAIDs and aspirin together increase the risk of gastrointestinal ulceration and bleeding. In addition, ibuprofen can occupy the platelet COX-1 site and block the irreversible effect of low-dose aspirin, which may reduce aspirin's cardioprotective benefit when ibuprofen is taken regularly. If ibuprofen is required, its timing relative to aspirin should be reviewed.

## Aspirin + clopidogrel (dual antiplatelet therapy)
Dual antiplatelet therapy is often intentional, for example after an acute coronary syndrome or coronary stent, but it increases bleeding risk. The indication and the planned duration should be confirmed and documented.

## Aspirin for primary prevention in older adults
The 2023 Beers Criteria recommend against starting aspirin for primary prevention of cardiovascular disease in older adults because the bleeding risk outweighs the benefit; continued use should be reviewed.
