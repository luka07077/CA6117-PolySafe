# Serotonergic drugs: serotonin syndrome and bleeding

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype. Sources: FDA prescribing information for tramadol (Ultram), sertraline (Zoloft), citalopram, escitalopram, fluoxetine (Prozac) and metoprolol, Warnings and Drug Interactions sections; STOPP/START version 3.

## SSRIs + tramadol
Tramadol has serotonergic activity in addition to its opioid effect. With an SSRI (sertraline, citalopram, escitalopram, fluoxetine) the risk of serotonin syndrome increases: agitation, confusion, tremor, hyperthermia, sweating and muscle rigidity. Tramadol also lowers the seizure threshold, and the risk is higher with antidepressants. A non-serotonergic analgesic should be considered, or the patient monitored closely.

## Tramadol + amitriptyline
Amitriptyline is a tricyclic antidepressant with serotonergic effects and also lowers the seizure threshold. With tramadol there is a risk of serotonin syndrome and seizures; patients should be monitored.

## SSRIs and bleeding
SSRIs deplete serotonin stored in platelets and impair platelet aggregation. Their labels warn of an increased risk of abnormal bleeding, especially when combined with NSAIDs, aspirin, warfarin or other anticoagulants. The combination of an SSRI and an NSAID increases the risk of upper gastrointestinal bleeding; gastroprotection or an alternative analgesic should be considered.

## Two SSRIs at the same time
Taking two SSRIs together is a therapeutic duplication that increases the risk of serotonin syndrome and bleeding without additional benefit. It often reflects an incomplete switch between antidepressants.

## Fluoxetine + metoprolol
Fluoxetine strongly inhibits CYP2D6, the main enzyme that metabolises metoprolol. Metoprolol levels can rise and cause bradycardia and hypotension. Heart rate and blood pressure should be monitored.
