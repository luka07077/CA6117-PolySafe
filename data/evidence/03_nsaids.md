# NSAIDs: gastrointestinal, kidney and blood-pressure risks

Summary written for the PolySafe prototype. Sources: FDA prescribing information for ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone and prednisone (Drug Interactions); Lapi F et al., "Concurrent use of diuretics, angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors, and angiotensin receptor blockers with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and risk of acute kidney injury", BMJ 2013;346:e8525; STOPP/START version 3 (Eur Geriatr Med 2023); AGS Beers Criteria 2023.

## NSAIDs in older adults
NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, celecoxib) increase the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding and peptic ulcer, acute kidney injury, fluid retention and raised blood pressure. The Beers Criteria advise avoiding chronic use unless other alternatives are not effective and the patient can take a gastroprotective agent.

## ACE inhibitors or ARBs + NSAIDs
NSAIDs reduce renal prostaglandin production. With an ACE inhibitor (lisinopril, ramipril, enalapril) or an ARB (losartan, valsartan) this blunts the blood-pressure-lowering effect and increases the risk of kidney injury, particularly in older or dehydrated patients. Blood pressure and kidney function should be monitored and long-term NSAID use avoided where possible.

## Triple whammy: ACE inhibitor or ARB + diuretic + NSAID
The combination of a renin-angiotensin system blocker, a diuretic (furosemide, hydrochlorothiazide or spironolactone) and an NSAID is known as the "triple whammy". In a large cohort study (Lapi et al., BMJ 2013) triple therapy was associated with a 31% higher rate of acute kidney injury, with the highest risk in the first 30 days of use. The NSAID should be avoided where possible and kidney function monitored.

## Spironolactone + NSAIDs
NSAIDs reduce kidney function and potassium excretion and can cause hyperkalaemia and kidney injury when combined with spironolactone. Potassium and kidney function should be monitored.

## Corticosteroids (prednisone, prednisolone) + NSAIDs
Systemic corticosteroids and NSAIDs together increase the risk of peptic ulcer and gastrointestinal bleeding. Gastroprotection should be considered if the combination is needed.

## Two NSAIDs at the same time (therapeutic duplication)
Taking two NSAIDs together (for example a prescribed naproxen plus over-the-counter ibuprofen) adds gastrointestinal and kidney toxicity without additional pain relief. This is a common unintentional duplication because NSAIDs are sold under many brand names (Advil, Motrin, Aleve, Voltaren).
